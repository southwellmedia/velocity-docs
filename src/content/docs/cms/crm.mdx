The CMS has a small CRM built in, so a site's forms lead somewhere. It needs no setup: the first person to send a
form becomes the first contact.

## What it holds

| | |
|---|---|
| **Contacts** | One per email address. Details, the team's notes and a timeline of everything the person sent and every stage they moved through. |
| **Pipeline** | The stages a contact moves through, as a board. A site renames, reorders, adds and removes stages. Each contact can have someone on the team who looks after it and a day to follow up. |
| **Deals** | What a contact may buy: an amount in the pipeline's currency, in a stage of the same pipeline. The Deals screen adds them up as open, forecast and won. |
| **Custom fields** | Fields a site adds to its contacts and its deals, such as "Membership type". |
| **Import** | Contacts from a CSV file. Someone already there only gains what they lack. |

Owners and editors can see and change contacts and deals. Authors cannot.

## How contacts are made

- **By a form.** A form that names its name and email fields makes a contact from each submission that is not
  spam. A form never overwrites what the team typed about a person: it only fills what is missing.
- **By hand**, under Contacts.
- **By import.** The editor reads the CSV in the browser and reports any row it could not take by its place in the
  file.

New contacts from forms can be given in turn to the people an owner names, to whoever of them looks after the fewest.

## Deals about something on the site

A content type can say that deals may be about its entries: listings, memberships, services. Set **Deals can be
about these** in the type builder, or `forDeals: true` in code. A deal then points at the entry itself, so it keeps
its subject when the entry is renamed, and the entry's screen lists the deals about it.

The forecast counts each open deal by its stage's chance of closing, which an owner sets per stage.

## Personal data

A contact is personal data, and the CMS treats it that way throughout.

- **Who can read it.** Only people whose role allows it. A plugin hears of contacts and submissions only if it
  declared that permission, which the owner sees before switching it on.
- **Where it never goes.** Not to a page, a cache, the search index or a log.
- **Export.** From a contact's screen, an owner or editor downloads a file of everything held about that person:
  details, notes, what they sent, their deals.
- **Erase.** Forgetting a person removes the contact, the notes, the deals and everything they sent. It cannot be
  undone.
- **Retention.** Each form can say how many days its submissions are kept.

<Aside type="caution" title="Not in the beta">
One pipeline per site. No reminder emails for follow-up days: what is due shows on the editor's home screen. No
email is sent from a contact's page. The words "Contacts" and "Deals" cannot be renamed.
</Aside>

## Sending contacts elsewhere

The **Webhooks** plugin posts what happens (a form sent, a contact or a deal changed) to addresses an owner lists.
The **Mailchimp**, **Slack** and **HubSpot** plugins are marked experimental: they pass their tests but have not
run against the real services. See [Writing a plugin](/cms/plugins/) for the ones that come with the CMS.
